## The three lines

This is the whole position.

1. **Finally make AI work for your business.**
2. **Use it properly.**
3. **Know you're doing everything you can with AI, ethically and safely.**

The second line is your "leverage AI properly", said in words the page can use. "Leverage" is on the banned list in the voice guide, so it stays off the site. The meaning stays.

The sentence an owner repeats:

> Finally make AI work for your business.

What they say to their partner:

> They'll finally make AI work for us, properly, and we'll know it's being done safely.

## What each line means

**Finally make AI work for your business.** The AI stops being a login and a good intention. It starts doing real work: the quote, the follow-up, the marketing, the admin that sits there.

**Use it properly.** The tools are connected. The data is in a shape AI can use. The jobs are chosen on purpose. A handful of chat logins is the before.

**Know you're doing everything you can, ethically and safely.** You've gone as far as is sensible. You could explain it to a client. Client data has a rule, and what goes out still sounds like the business. The feeling is yours. The rule itself is still `[CONFIRM: where data lives, whose accounts, what never goes into a model, whether you approve what goes out]`. Until that paragraph exists, the page can promise the stance and cannot describe the mechanism.

## What the page says, in order

One idea each. Short.

1. **Hero.** Finally make AI work for your business.
2. **The two promises under it.** Properly. And everything you can, ethically and safely.
3. **Where it goes.** The quote, the follow-up, the marketing, the admin. The portal when the data needs a home.
4. **How it works.** A call. A roadmap with the numbers. Then the build, one job at a time, a month to prove it.
5. **The fee.** A$3,500 a month, including GST. AI running costs are billed to the client, separately. No contract.
6. **Dan.** Named. Flexfolio and ReviewBuddy run on these systems.
7. **Close.** Book a call.

The roadmap, the price and "you keep what we built" are why the decision feels sane. They are not the reason someone stops scrolling.

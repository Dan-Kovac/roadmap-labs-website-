## 1. The read

The buyer is already clear. An owner, still in the day-to-day, with a team of about five to ten and sometimes more. They know AI should be in the business. It isn't, because there has not been a spare week. The tools sit apart, the data is not in a shape anything can use, and they will not put client information somewhere they don't understand. Under that sit the older symptoms: quotes on a Sunday night, follow-ups that go cold, marketing that never starts, a VA who needs briefing, a few thousand a month in tools that do a fraction of their job.

The feeling to create is also clear: relief, confidence, a real effect on the business, the sense of being current, and the sense that this is the right move now. Those five don't belong in one headline. They belong in different parts of the page.

What has been weak is the join. The admin drafts made the fee feel sensible and the ambition feel small. The first AI pass reached for the ambition and could have been written by any agency, with "properly" doing the work of a method. The page lands when three things are true at once:

1. AI is actually going into the business, on jobs the owner can name.
2. A$3,500 a month makes sense against money they already spend.
3. The decision can be undone.

Ambition leads. The fee is the sense-check, lower on the page. The undo sits next to the button.

Being current has to come through in the order of the work, the limits we state, and the fact that Dan runs his own companies on these systems. It cannot be a claim. "Two months ahead", "cutting-edge", "the newest", "stop falling behind" and "without having to keep up" are the same claim in different clothes. Leave them all out.

### Answers that don't need another conversation

- **The sentence an owner repeats:** "They get AI working in the business, and we see the numbers before we pay."
- **The promise to hold:** AI working in the business, on jobs that matter, with the thinking done for them. Setup is how. Impact is what they feel. The roadmap is why they can say yes.
- **Cause, then symptoms.** The first screen is the reason AI isn't in the business yet. Quotes, follow-ups, quiet marketing and half-used tools are how they recognise themselves, one section down, and the examples of where the work goes.
- **"Set up" as a picture.** The tools they already pay for connected. Data in fields with the same names, so AI can use it. A written rule for what client data can go where. Then one job moved. The rule waits on Dan. Until it exists, the page can name the worry and cannot claim it is handled.
- **Impact, in their words.** The quote goes out when it's asked for. The follow-up happens. Marketing goes out without an agency. The owner stops being the person who holds it all in their head.
- **Category.** Studio. Owners understand it as small, and as the person who does the work. The phrase they will actually say is "someone to get AI working here." Inventing a search category ("AI operating partner", "in-house AI team") puts us in the crowd we need to stay out of.
- **How we stay out of that crowd.** Publish the price. Show the order of work. Say what we will not promise. Name Dan. Talk about quotes, data and a one-month test. Refuse the adjective list.
- **What being current looks like, without a claim.** He knows what comes first (a job that proves itself, then the portal). He prices the model costs into the fee and says so. He shows estimates with the working, and he switches a build off. Later, his own writing teaches the setup before the tactic. The site can carry the method, the fee and the companies. It cannot carry a speed claim.
- **Proof, in order.** The roadmap method. The terms (published price, one-month test, no contract, handover). Dan, named. Flexfolio and ReviewBuddy running on Roadmap Labs systems. The agency years. Compass only with written permission. No results slot until there is a result.
- **"Tested on our own first"** stays off the site. The confirmed fact is smaller and still useful: Flexfolio and ReviewBuddy run on Roadmap Labs systems. Which jobs, and whether new models are tried there before a client sees them, are still open.
- **Safety** is a reassurance beside the offer, written as a fact, once Dan can say where data lives, whose accounts the work runs on, and what never goes into a model. It is a hole in the page until then.
- **Dan on the page.** Name, a one-line founder note, email in the close. A photo if he is happy to use one. A small handful of clients is the capacity line. The Sportsbet role belongs in one plain sentence on About, because buyers will open LinkedIn. Hiding it makes the handful-of-clients line look evasive.
- **First proof to add later.** A client's roadmap estimates set beside what actually happened, with their OK. Leave room in the work list. Leave the slot empty now.
- **Name of the offer.** None. "One monthly fee" in public lines. "Retainer" once, in the same breath as "no contract, cancel any month." Calling the fee "The Roadmap" collides with step 2 and with the studio name.
- **What step 2 is called.** A roadmap. "Proposal" sounds like a pitch.
- **Hero of the page vs hero of the offer.** The page opens on the outcome. The offer opens on the roadmap, because that is what makes A$3,500 feel like a decision they can look at. The free session is the first step, and it should not be the headline. A headline about a free session reads as a lead magnet.
- **The call.** Treat "Book a call" as the free discovery session until Dan says there is a shorter fit chat first. Two steps before a useful conversation feels like a sales process.
- **Who it's for, on the page.** "Owner-led" does the work. Keep "5 to 10 or more" as internal guidance. A number in the hero excludes a good buyer and invites a firm that wants a procurement process. Stay broad by industry. Quotes, follow-ups and marketing let a buyers agency and a business that quotes both see themselves. Compass is proof, with permission. It is not the niche.
- **A$3,500.** It lives in the offer. About A$808 a week can sit under the price as a supporting line. The comparison (SEO about A$2,000, ad-management fees about A$1,000, a VA about A$2,170, together about A$5,170, gap about A$1,670) sits under the fee as Dan's benchmarks, labelled as such. Use A$1,670 until the GST basis is confirmed. The table is a sense-check. It is not the reason to buy.
- **Insights.** Hidden until three articles exist. The writing is the long proof of being current. Dan writes it. Topics are listed at the end as titles only.

### What I would ask you to change in the raw framing

Keep the buyer. Keep the five feelings, and give them different jobs on the page. Retire every line that says or implies a race. Keep "set up" as the method and take it out of the headline. Hold safety out of the promise until it is a fact. Keep the cost comparison, move it down, and label it.

## 2. Positioning

### Recommended

**The sentence**

Roadmap Labs gets AI working in the business, and you see the numbers before you commit.

**What they say to their partner**

They get AI working in the business, and we see the numbers before we pay.

**The paragraph**

Roadmap Labs is for owners who know AI should be part of how the business runs, and have not had a week to get it there. The tools don't connect, the data is not in a shape AI can use, and there is no agreed rule for client data. Roadmap Labs does the thinking and the building. It gets the tools and the data into a state AI can work from, then puts AI on the jobs that change the week: the quote, the follow-up, the report, the marketing that should be going out. One job at a time, kept if it works, switched off if it doesn't. Before the monthly fee, the owner sees a roadmap of what each job could save or earn, when it would be running, and when it should pay back. Estimates, with the working shown. Dan Kovac runs Flexfolio and ReviewBuddy on these systems, and the studio takes a small handful of clients.

### Alternative: relief in the sentence

**The sentence**

Roadmap Labs takes on the AI work you don't have a week for, and shows you the numbers first.

This one is warmer and closer to how the owner feels on a Sunday. It is also easier to confuse with a freelancer or a VA, and the method (setup, then the job) disappears from the line they remember. Use this tone in the recognition section. Keep the recommended sentence as the one they repeat.

### Alternative: setup in the sentence

**The sentence**

Roadmap Labs gets your tools and data ready for AI, then puts it on one job at a time.

This is the most precise, and it is the version that already fell flat as a headline. "Ready" and "set up" are means. The owner wants the business using AI. Use this as the explanation under the headline, in the section on how the work is done.

## 3. Message hierarchy

Built for the recommended sentence. If the relief sentence is chosen, the recognition section has to work harder to show the method. If the setup sentence is chosen, the hero has to work harder to show the effect on the week.

### Value proposition

Roadmap Labs gets AI working in the business, and you see the numbers before you commit.

### Supporting points

1. **The business is made usable first.** Tools connected. Data in a shape AI can actually read. The company portal comes when there is something to put in it, usually about a month of work, on the same fee. Clients do not have to start there.
2. **Then one real job.** A quote, a follow-up, onboarding, a report, or the marketing that isn't going out. Built around how the team already works.
3. **A month to earn its place.** Each automation is tested for a month and switched off if it isn't working.
4. **Dan runs it on his own companies.** Flexfolio and ReviewBuddy run on Roadmap Labs systems. That is the confirmed line. Anything stronger waits.

Safety is not a supporting point yet. The moment it can be said in one concrete paragraph, it slots in beside point 1.

### Risk reversal

Use the line already approved for draft:

> Before you commit, you see what each automation could save, when it'll be running and when it should pay back. The most you can spend finding out after that is one month, and you keep what we built.

Mark the last clause `[CONFIRM: handover]` until the terms say they keep the work, the documentation and the accounts.

Next to the button:

> No pressure. Look over the numbers, then decide.

If Dan will say so when the numbers don't stack up, add one line to the roadmap step: "If the numbers don't stack up, we'll say so." That line does more for trust than any adjective. It stays off the page until he agrees he will actually say it.

### Call to action

One action: **Book a call.**

It is a conversation about the service and what would be worth doing. Email Dan only in the closing band. The live line "Tell Dan what has to happen." stays with that email.

### Category

**AI studio.** Footer descriptor, proposed: "AI studio · Melbourne" (well under 40 characters).

"Software & automations studio" describes the craft list. The audit already flagged that list. "Agency" is the word this buyer has been burnt by.

### Offer

No product name.

Public name for the commercial thing: **one monthly fee.**

Name for step 2: **a roadmap with the numbers.**

## 4. How the homepage should be ordered

One idea per section. The designer has said the layout can move. Grey-then-ink pairs on every section will read as a template, so the recognition section and the founder note should break the pattern.

| Order | Section | The one idea |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hero | AI can be working in this business, and you can see the numbers before you commit. Who it's for sits in the eyebrow or the lead: owner-led, Melbourne. No price. |
| 2 | Recognition (new) | Four signs the business isn't set up for it yet: no spare week, tools that don't connect, data AI can't use, no rule for what's safe. |
| 3 | Where it goes | The jobs, in concrete nouns. The portal as the later step, when the data needs a home. |
| 4 | How it works | Discovery, the roadmap, the build. "No pressure" beside the button. |
| 5 | The fee | A$3,500 a month, incl. GST and AI running costs. What the fee includes. The benchmark table under it, labelled as benchmarks. |
| 6 | Dan | Named. The agency years. The two companies. A small handful of clients. |
| 7 | Close | Book a call. Tell Dan what has to happen. |

Insights stays hidden. Selected work can sit after Dan, or inside the Dan section as three named rows, once Compass is cleared. Empty proof stays off the page. A fake metric, a logo wall or a testimonial slot would undo the calm.

Services and pricing holds the long version of sections 4 and 5, plus the fine print and the FAQ. Contact, About and Insights are rewritten to this position in the next pass, after the sentence is agreed.

### Lines already good enough to keep

- "See the numbers. Then we build."
- "No pressure. Look over the numbers, then decide."
- "Tell Dan what has to happen."
- "Are the savings guaranteed? No." followed by the plain statement that they are estimates.
- "Book a call."
- The live holding line, "Building software has never been easier. Building the right software is still just as hard.", moves to About or to a future note. It speaks to builders. The buyer needs the other sentence.

### Hero, when we write it

The first line should be a situation an owner recognises (they know AI matters, and the week to work it out isn't there). The second line should be the outcome (it gets working in the business) or the safety of the decision (the numbers come first). One concrete noun in the lead. The lead can name the free session. It should not recite "software, automations and marketing automations."

No hero options in this document. Writing them now would spend the lines before the sentence is agreed.

## 6. Decisions that block the page copy

In the order they should be answered. A yes or a no on each is enough.

1. **The sentence.** Recommended, relief, or setup. Or rewrite it in the margin.
2. **Safety, in one paragraph you would say out loud.** Where does data live, whose accounts does the work run on, what never goes into a model, and do they approve marketing before it goes out? If the practice is still open, say so, and the page will name the worry without answering it. A line we can use the day it's true: "We agree what data can go where before we connect anything."
3. **Own companies.** Are new models and tools tried on Flexfolio and ReviewBuddy before a client sees them? If yes, which jobs can be named? If no, the site stays on "they run on Roadmap Labs systems."
4. **The call.** Is the booked call the discovery session itself?
5. **The roadmap.** Is it free, and do they keep it if they don't continue? If the numbers don't stack up, will you say don't start?
6. **Handover.** Can the site say "you keep what we built"? Docs, accounts, notice, who pays from when.
7. **About.** Name Sportsbet, or describe the product role without the company? Is "ten years" the line?
8. **Benchmarks.** Are the SEO, ad-fee and VA figures inc. GST or ex GST?
9. **Compass.** May the site name Compass Buyers Agency and Compass OS?
10. **Included AI costs.** Is there a fair-use cap we should say in the fine print, or does "including AI running costs" stand alone for now?

Everything else in the brief's open list can wait for the page-copy pass.

## 7. Notes for the copy, once this is agreed

- Plain speech, the way Dan talks to a client across a table. Short sentences. Contractions. One idea a line.
- Australian spelling. Sentence case, including the button. Prices as "A$3,500/mo incl. GST".
- No em or en dashes. No exclamation marks.
- "We" for the studio. "Dan" when it's him. Never both in one sentence.
- Concrete nouns. Quotes, follow-ups, the CRM, the portal, the roadmap, the month.
- One everyday image in a whole page, if any, and follow it through. The margherita-pizza image belongs to the LinkedIn note. It doesn't belong on the homepage.
- AI is a tool for judgement and for getting a job done. Say the job.
- Say who it's for in the first screen.
- Estimates stay estimates. The site never shows a saving, a revenue figure or an ROI as if it happened.
- One "Book a call". Reassurance in the same view as the button.
- Banned, and worth repeating because the raw brief used several of them: unlock, unleash, elevate, empower, seamless, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, next-level, leverage (as a verb), streamline, supercharge, harness, journey, ecosystem as filler, "AI-powered", solutions, passionate, game-changer, robust. Also "guaranteed ROI", "no-brainer", "pays for itself."
- Also out: rule-of-three padding, a string of rhetorical questions, a zinger at the end of every section, and the same idea said three ways.

### Article titles, for when Insights opens

Dan writes the pieces. These are angles that show the order of work and the limits, which is the current-ness the page is not allowed to claim.

1. What to sort out before you point AI at the business.
2. Why the first build is a single job, and the portal comes later.
3. What AI costs to run, and why that cost sits inside the fee.
4. Every automation gets a month.
5. Write for the person reading. The algorithm can wait.

The live line about building the right software can be the sixth, filed under About or under notes, when he wants to write it.

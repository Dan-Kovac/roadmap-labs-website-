When you're pricing marketing help, the monthly fee is the easy number to compare. The harder question is who actually does the work.

A fractional CMO, an agency and an in-house hire can all land somewhere near each other on price. What you get for the money is very different. Here's what each one costs in Australia, using published prices where they exist, and what you're paying for.

## What is a fractional CMO?

A fractional CMO is a senior marketing leader you hire part-time, usually for a set number of hours or days a month. They own the marketing strategy and the plan, and often manage whoever does the work.

You'll also see it called a virtual or part-time CMO. As Melbourne fractional CMO [Shanna Gualtieri puts it](https://shannagualtieri.com/fractional-cmo-melbourne), they "all describe the same role".

The appeal is simple. You get senior thinking without a senior salary.

## What a fractional CMO costs in Australia

These are published prices, checked on each provider's own page in October 2026:

- **Z10 Consulting:** "Engagements start at A$3,500 a month for 20 hours of senior marketing leadership." That works out to about A$175 an hour. ([Z10 Consulting](https://z10consulting.com.au/services/fractional-cmo/melbourne/))
- **Shanna Gualtieri, Melbourne:** retainers from $4,000 + GST a month. Her day rate is $1,500 + GST, and one day a week "works out to about $6,500 + GST a month". ([shannagualtieri.com](https://shannagualtieri.com/fractional-cmo-melbourne))
- **rambert.co, Melbourne:** advisory "starts from $2,450 a month", where they set the direction and review the work and your team implements it. A hands-on engagement "runs $6,500 to $14,500 a month", where they lead the plan and manage its delivery. ([rambert.co](https://rambert.co/services/fractional-cmo/))

Two things to check when you compare them. Some prices are quoted plus GST and some don't say. And the cheaper end is usually advisory, which means your own team still does the work.

Shanna's page also says she'll "brief, manage and hold your agencies and freelancers to account". That's a useful service. It also tells you that the agencies and freelancers are often a separate bill.

## How much a marketing agency costs

Agency pricing is harder to pin down, because most agencies quote per job and few publish their prices.

From my years running an agency, these are typical monthly figures for a small business:

- A well-run SEO campaign: about A$2,000 a month.
- Managing Meta, Google or social ads: about A$1,000 a month. That's the agency's management fee only. The ad spend sits on top.

The thing to know about agencies is that they usually run one channel. Your SEO agency does SEO. Your ads agency does ads. Someone in the business still has to brief them, join the dots and check the results, and in a team of five or ten that's usually the owner.

Add some admin help on top, like a virtual assistant at A$500 or more a week (about A$2,170 a month), and the stack gets to roughly A$5,170 a month.

## What an in-house marketing hire costs

[SEEK's salary data](https://www.seek.com.au/career-advice/role/marketing-manager/salary) puts the national median base salary for a marketing manager at $120,000 a year, with the middle half of advertised roles between $110,000 and $130,000.

For a [marketing coordinator](https://www.seek.com.au/career-advice/role/marketing-coordinator/salary), a more junior role, SEEK's median is $80,000, with the middle half between $75,000 and $85,000.

On top of that you pay super. The [super guarantee](https://www.ato.gov.au/tax-rates-and-codes/key-superannuation-rates-and-thresholds/super-guarantee) is 12%.

So, roughly:

- A marketing manager is about $10,000 a month in base salary, or about $11,200 with super.
- A marketing coordinator is about $6,670 a month, or about $7,470 with super.

Treat those as a rough guide. SEEK's figures come from salary ranges employers put in job ads, and some of those ranges include super while others don't. Recruitment, leave, software and the time it takes someone to get up to speed aren't in them either.

The upside is a person who's yours, every day, who gets to know the business properly. The catch is that one person can only be good at so many things. A great content marketer isn't always the person you want running your Google Ads.

| In-house marketing manager | About $10,000 a month base, plus super (SEEK) | Them, across whatever they're good at |

## Which one fits a small business

There's no single right answer. It depends on what's missing.

If you need a senior person in your leadership meetings who owns the marketing plan and the budget, that's what a fractional CMO is for. Pick one whose price includes the amount of time you actually need.

If you know exactly which channel you need and just want it run well, an agency for that channel makes sense.

If there's enough work for a full-time person, and someone in the business to manage them, an in-house hire gives you the most day-to-day attention.

If you've got the direction but nobody to do the work, that's the gap most small businesses are actually sitting in.
